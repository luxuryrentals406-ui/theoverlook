Montana Wedding Venues

Choosing a wedding venue in Montana

Most Montana venues fall into two kinds: a space you rent for a day, and a property you take over for a weekend. Here is how they differ and what to ask.

The rented venue

You get the site for a block of hours. Tables, chairs, power and a bar are often rented and delivered, and guests stay somewhere else. The venue fee is usually lower, with more vendors to coordinate.

The estate buyout

You book the whole property. The celebration and the lodging are on the same land, the power, tent and bar are already in place, and the weekend is yours from the rehearsal to the last morning.

Six questions to ask any venue

Is the power permanent?

If not, generators have to be rented and placed for the weekend. Here it is 200 AMP permanently installed service, with water on site.

How far do vendors carry everything?

Load-in distance affects how long setup takes. Here vendors can pull up within 50 feet of the venue.

Where does everyone park?

Seventy-five cars can park on the property.

When does the music stop?

Ask for the end time before you book. Contracts here are written to an 11:00 p.m. event end.

What is already standing?

A 3,200 sq ft pavilion, a 40 × 80 ft tent, a built-in bar, tables and chairs including four head tables, and a fire pit.

Does another event share the weekend?

One group is on this property at a time, with no shared facilities.
