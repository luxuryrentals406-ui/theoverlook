What’s included in an estate-buyout wedding venue

A traditional venue rents you a space for a few hours. A buyout gives you the whole property for your stay.

September 2, 2026 · The Overlook at Flathead Lake

Most wedding venues rent you a space for a set block of hours. An estate buyout works differently: you book the whole property, every building and every acre, for the length of your stay.

Here is what that means at The Overlook, and how it compares to a traditional venue rental.

The whole property, only yours

The Overlook sits on 15 private acres above Flathead Lake. When you book, the property is not shared with any other party or event. It is yours for the length of your stay.

That makes the day simpler. Getting ready happens in one of the houses on site. First looks can happen wherever the light is best. There is no other wedding sharing the parking or the grounds.

Your closest people sleep where the wedding happens

The biggest difference is lodging. The estate sleeps up to 28 guests in five accommodations, all on the same fifteen acres:

  • The Swan — the four-bedroom main house.
  • The Glacier — a cabin with a sleeping loft and its own patio.
  • The Lakeside — a pair of modern tiny homes.
  • The Summit and The Ridge — two treehouses in the trees.

Your wedding party and closest family can stay where the wedding happens. Nobody in that group needs a shuttle or a late drive back to a hotel, and everyone wakes up in the same place the next morning.

Space built for the celebration itself

The estate hosts receptions for up to 200 guests in a 3,200 square-foot pavilion and a 40 by 80 ft tent. Both are permanent, so nothing has to be delivered and set up for your weekend.

Around them are a pool and hot tub, open lawn for the ceremony and lawn games, and views of the lake and mountains.

Tables and chairs are on site, including four head tables, and the bar is built in.

A weekend, not a time slot

With a buyout you have the property for your whole stay, not a few hours on one day. The rehearsal dinner, a welcome gathering, the wedding and breakfast the next morning can all happen in the same place.

Quiet hours, vendor access and your timeline are set out in your agreement. Contracts are written to a 200-guest maximum and an 11:00 p.m. event end.

What you still bring in

Couples at The Overlook work with a day-of coordinator (required), carry event insurance, and arrange restroom rentals for larger guest counts. Catering is arranged separately. We keep a preferred vendor list of Flathead Valley planners, caterers, florists and photographers who know the property, and several extend a partner discount, but you are not required to book from it. A full planner is available if you’d rather hand the details to someone else entirely.

Is a buyout right for you?

If you want a single evening with as few moving parts as possible, a traditional venue may suit you better. If you want your family and friends in one place for a few days, a buyout is built for that.
