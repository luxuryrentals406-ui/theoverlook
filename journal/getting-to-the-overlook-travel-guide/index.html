Getting to The Overlook: a travel guide for wedding guests

The logistics you can hand straight to your guest list, from the airport to the last shuttle of the night.

September 2, 2026 · The Overlook at Flathead Lake

A destination wedding asks something of your guests, and the kindest thing you can do is make the logistics feel easy. The good news: getting to The Overlook is simpler than most Montana destinations. Here’s the guide to share with your guest list.

Fly into Glacier Park International (FCA)

The closest airport is Glacier Park International Airport in Kalispell, Montana — airport code FCA. From FCA, the estate is roughly a 35-minute drive. That’s the single most useful fact to put on your wedding website, because it tells guests immediately that they won’t be spending half a day in a car after landing.

FCA is a small, easy airport: short walks, quick baggage claim, rental counters right there. Encourage anyone arriving for the wedding day itself to build in buffer — small regional airports leave less room to recover from a missed connection.

Renting a car vs. arranging shuttles

Both work. Which is right depends on your guest list.

Rental cars make sense when guests are arriving on different days, want to explore the valley on their own schedule, or are extending the trip. Rentals at FCA are limited in peak season, so tell guests to reserve early — earlier than feels necessary. It’s the most common travel regret we hear.

Group shuttles make sense when a large block of guests arrives in a similar window, when you’d rather not manage a parking lot full of cars, or — most importantly — when there’s a bar. A shuttle for the wedding evening is the simplest way to make sure nobody drives after the reception. Many couples arrange a rental car for a handful of key people and a shuttle for everyone else.

  • Book shuttle service well ahead; regional operators fill up in summer.
  • Give the driver one point of contact from your side, not five.
  • Plan the last shuttle for after your music curfew, not at it.
  • Share the parking situation in advance — the estate accommodates onsite parking for 75 cars.

Where guests stay

The estate sleeps 28 guests onsite across the main house, the cabin, the tiny homes and the treehouses — typically reserved for family and the wedding party. Everyone else stays nearby in the Lakeside and Flathead Valley area, and we’re glad to point you toward partner accommodations so you can send guests a short, curated list rather than an overwhelming one.

Extending the trip

Many guests turn a wedding weekend here into a proper Montana trip, and the estate is a natural home base for it. Flathead Lake itself is the obvious draw — boating, swimming, waterfront dining, and cherries in late summer. The valley also offers alpine adventure, hiking, and small-town Montana worth an unhurried afternoon.

Glacier National Park is the other great reason to stay longer. West Glacier, the park’s west entrance, is roughly a one-hour drive from the estate — about 49 miles. That’s close enough for a day in the park and back, though guests should plan a full day for it rather than squeezing it around wedding events, and should check the park’s own website before going, since entry requirements and park conditions change from year to year.

A simple note to send your guests

Feel free to borrow this: “Fly into Glacier Park International Airport (FCA) in Kalispell, Montana — the venue is about 35 minutes away. Reserve a rental car early if you’d like to explore, and watch for shuttle details for the wedding evening. If you can, stay an extra day or two: Flathead Lake is right here, and West Glacier — the west entrance to Glacier National Park — is about an hour’s drive, roughly 49 miles.”
